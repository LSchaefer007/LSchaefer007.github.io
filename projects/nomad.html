MOBILE ROBOTICS · MECHANICAL DESIGN · ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING

AXIS Nomad

A compact, modular mobile robotics platform being developed as a physical endpoint for AXIS. Current development is focused on mechanical packaging, design for additive manufacturing, Raspberry Pi integration, and creating a serviceable internal architecture for future robotics hardware.

Fusion 360 DfAM Mechanical Packaging Raspberry Pi Robotics Rapid Prototyping
STATUS Early Development
PRIMARY CAD Fusion 360
MANUFACTURING FDM 3D Printing
COMPUTE PLATFORM Raspberry Pi
01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Designing a compact robotics platform from the inside out.

Nomad began as an effort to create a compact physical platform capable of eventually extending AXIS into the real world. Rather than treating the enclosure as a shell built around finished electronics, the mechanical design is being developed around the requirements of the individual subsystems.

The current work focuses on fitting the drivetrain, camera, speakers, computing hardware, power electronics, and supporting structures into a compact enclosure while keeping the design modular and suitable for additive manufacturing.

The project is still in early development. The mechanical CAD shown here represents the current design direction and should not be interpreted as a completed autonomous robot.

02 — DESIGN GOALS

Mechanical requirements

The enclosure and internal architecture are being designed around a small set of core requirements.

01

Compact

Package multiple mechanical and electronic subsystems within a small mobile platform.

02

Modular

Allow individual mounts and internal structures to be replaced or redesigned without rebuilding the entire chassis.

03

3D Printable

Develop the primary mechanical architecture around additive manufacturing and rapid design iteration.

04

Serviceable

Maintain access to internal hardware for assembly, maintenance, troubleshooting, and future upgrades.

03 — CHASSIS ARCHITECTURE

The mechanical foundation

Removing the exterior shell reveals the mounting structure beneath the finished form.

Lower Chassis & Internal Structure

The lower chassis provides the structural foundation for the Nomad mechanical system. Internal posts, mounting locations, walls, openings, and clearance geometry provide defined locations for subsystem hardware while supporting the removable upper structure.

04 — SUBSYSTEM PACKAGING

Hardware-specific mounting

Instead of treating every internal component as a generic block, Nomad uses mounting structures designed around specific hardware.

COMPONENT HOLDERS

Subsystem mounting structures

Mounting structures were developed for the speakers, Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 Wide NoIR, and drivetrain motors. These components establish repeatable positions for hardware inside the chassis.

INTEGRATION

Mounts integrated into the chassis

The individual mounting structures are positioned inside the lower chassis to evaluate alignment, available volume, component relationships, and interference with surrounding structures.

05 — COMPUTE PACKAGING
RASPBERRY PI INTEGRATION

Using vertical space

The Raspberry Pi mounting structure sits above lower-mounted subsystems, allowing Nomad to use the available vertical volume instead of placing every component on a single plane.

This layered layout helps separate major subsystem groups while preserving the compact exterior dimensions of the platform.

06 — POWER PACKAGING
POWER CONVERSION

Dedicated power-electronics mounting

A dedicated buck converter mounting location was incorporated into the internal architecture rather than leaving the power electronics unsupported inside the enclosure.

Providing defined locations for power, compute, drivetrain, camera, and audio hardware helps make the overall system easier to assemble and refine as the design develops.

07 — CURRENT MECHANICAL DESIGN

Current printable architecture

The current mechanical assembly combines the main structures developed so far into one internal packaging system.

CURRENTLY DESIGNED

3D-printable components

The current CAD assembly includes the lower chassis, component mounting structures, Raspberry Pi mounting structure, buck converter holder, upper electronics deck, and supporting internal geometry.

DESIGN APPROACH

Modular additive manufacturing

The internal architecture is being split into replaceable printed components so individual structures can be modified and reprinted as hardware requirements change.
